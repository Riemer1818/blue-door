# Jenny Bakker — tooling and pain points

**Source:** WhatsApp, 2026-06-16 17:15–17:16. Carried over from `lintel/notes/lab-workflows.md`.
Short exchange, not a structured interview.

## Observations

- **eLABjournal** is handy for the **strain collection** and for **sharing protocols**.
- Her actual **lab journal** lives mostly in **OneNote**.
- For **growth experiments** she makes **Word files** and stores the data in **Excel files** on
  **OneDrive**.
- Biggest annoyance with eLAB: **barely usable on the phone**, and a lot of things
  *"precies niet soepel"* — they just don't work smoothly.

## Takeaways

- **Tooling is fragmented by function, not by choice.** One system for strains and protocols
  (eLABjournal), another for the journal (OneNote), another for data (Word/Excel on OneDrive).
  Nobody designed this stack; it accreted around the friction.
- **People route around the official ELN when it is clunky.** The ELN did not lose on features.
  It lost the journal — its own core use case — to OneNote, on smoothness.
- **Mobile is a real gap.** At the bench you have gloves on and a phone in your pocket, not a
  laptop.

## What this moves

- Supports **capture-not-chat** ([../README.md](../README.md)): the failure is at the point of
  entry, and it is severe enough that the record itself migrates elsewhere.
- Supports thesis #6 in [../theses.md](../theses.md) — entrenched personal workflows.
- **Direct evidence against an EU incumbent.** eLABjournal is eLabNext, which merged into
  **SciSure**, the European roll-up in our [landscape](../../market/eln-landscape/README.md).
  This is a first-hand pain report against the specific vendor holding the ground we would want.

## Open questions for a follow-up

- What would she have to be able to do on the phone for the journal to stay in the ELN?
- Does the strain collection stay in eLAB because it is good, or because it is shared and
  therefore nobody can unilaterally leave?
- Who chose eLABjournal, and could she have refused it?
