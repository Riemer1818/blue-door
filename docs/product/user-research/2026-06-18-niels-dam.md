# Niels Dam — AI tooling and where the data lives

**Source:** conversation, 2026-06-18. Carried over from `lintel/notes/lab-workflows.md`.

## On using AI with research data

- **No appetite for handing research data to big/external AI.** Privacy is a real blocker.
- Uses **"academic AI"** instead — *the university pays for it*, and it is **processed within the
  EU**.
- Has not come across **MCP** yet. Has seen **online search** as a feature.
- The academic-AI portal is a **whole platform**: chatting, building custom AIs, and a "lab"
  section for adjusting AI settings.

## Where the data lives

- **Data lives in the chat**, though you can feed the bot data.
- So far mostly used for **plots** — e.g. *"vierkantjes in het gras"* (quadrats in grass) → a
  greenness index via R — and **converting file formats**.
- I.e. mostly **data wrangling, not analysis**.
- Also runs **LM Studio locally**, on a 32 GB machine.

## Electronic lab journal

- The ELN is **where the "big guns" of your PhD live** — the serious record.
- **Not easily searchable for an LLM.** He is experimenting with keeping **Obsidian notes** to
  work around this.
- There is also "a sort of data platform" attached.

## Protocols and storage

- **Protocol sharing** happens over **OneDrive**.
- Storage generally: **OneDrive, personal storage, Nextcloud** — specifically
  https://thegood.cloud/ (new to us).
- Idea he floated unprompted: **have an API**, so something could read the data in.

## Adoption

- **Varies a lot** whether people use AI tools at all.
- One colleague uses **ChatGPT + DeepSeek** in combination.
- The **academic AI is not yet widely used**.
- Verdict: *"as a university it would be worth it."*

## Takeaways

- **Privacy plus EU processing is the unlock**, and there is a working proof of it: institutional
  EU-hosted "academic AI" gets used on real data precisely because of who hosts it. This is the
  strongest single piece of evidence we have for the EU-sovereignty wedge, and it is mechanistic
  rather than a preference — he is not choosing EU, he is refusing non-EU.
- **Current AI use is shallow.** Wrangling, plots, format conversion — not analysis. Room to move
  up the value chain, but also a warning: the demand that exists today is unglamorous.
- **The ELN holds the crown-jewel data and is not LLM-accessible.** People hand-roll workarounds.
  Searchability, or an API over the ELN, is a clear and self-identified gap.
- **He asked for an API unprompted.** That is a different product shape from a new ELN: a layer
  over the record someone already keeps, rather than a replacement for it.

## What this moves

- Strongly supports **EU data sovereignty** as the wedge ([../README.md](../README.md)) — with an
  actual mechanism, not just a compliance argument.
- **Complicates capture-not-chat.** Jenny's problem is that data never gets in. Niels's problem is
  that it *is* in and cannot be got out. Those are opposite failure modes and they may not be the
  same product. See the contradiction noted in [../theses.md](../theses.md).
- **Adds a competitor category the market scan missed entirely**: university-provided AI portals.
  They are free at the point of use, already EU-hosted, and already trusted. See
  [../../market/eln-landscape/README.md](../../market/eln-landscape/README.md).
- Local inference (LM Studio, 32 GB) suggests appetite for self-hosted/on-prem is real in this
  segment, not just a procurement checkbox.

## Open questions for a follow-up

- Would he give data to an EU-hosted startup, or does the trust come from the *university* being
  the counterparty rather than from the hosting location?
- What does he search the ELN *for* when it fails him?
